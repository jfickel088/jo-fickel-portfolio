# User research 

## Target audience

My target audience for this project is policy makers in San Antonio. My primary goal is to showcase the ways in which heat is a real and persistent problem for many individuals in South-Central Texas and will only continue to worsen as time goes on due to climate change and the urban heat island effect. 

## Interview script

My goals with the peer interviews were mainly concerned with subject matter familiarity, the clarity of the messaging, and the overall flow of the medium. Additionally, I was identify individuals with different levels of background knowledge which allowed me to explore ways to make my project more accessible to audiences with varying amounts of familiarity.

| Goal | Questions to Ask |
|------|------------------|
| Ascertain subject matter knowledge | Did you have any familiarity with the subject matter prior to reviewing the presentation? Do you feel like you know more about urban heat now than before? |
| Message efficacy | Based on the presentation, would you say that my audience and narrative are clear? Do my visualizations aid in telling the story? Should I add any additional visualizations? |
| Understand how to improve the flow of the story and/or presentation | Did the flow of the presentation make sense? Did any questions arise about the subject matter? Do you feel like anything was missing or could have been explored further? Were the visual elements readable? |

## Interview findings

| Questions               | Interview 1 | Interview 2 | Interview 3 |
|-------------------------|--------------------------------|-------------|-------------|
| Do you feel like you know more about urban heat now than before? | Already somewhat familiar with the material, but the presentation went into more specifics regarding the role of redlining and differing impacts | Completely new to the topic, but felt that information presented was fairly thorough; presentation needs to be built out more | Limited prior knowldge, but acknowledged the importance of the issue and interest in learning more based on initial presentation |
| Would you say that my audience and narrative are clear? | The final message and call to action were obvious: "re-evaluate and re-fund climate programs" | The title was helpful in setting the stage for the rest of the presentation; audience and overarching narrative is clear based on the highlighting of misaligned goals and expectations by the city (verbally), but visualizations should be utilized more often and earlier in the presentation | The amount of text showcases the amount of knowledge the author has regarding the topic as well as the amount of research put into the project; however, more visual elements should be incorporated for presentation purposes since time is limited |
| Do you feel like anything was missing or could have been explored further? | More visual elements and eye-catching components need to be added prior to finalization  | Make it feel more personal to the audience by highlighting the human elements (i.e., highlight the impact of heat on health outcomes) | Another visualization about green space coverage throughout the city would better tie in the call to action with the rest of the information about effective solutions |
| Did the flow of the presentation make sense? Were the visual elements readable? | Wants to introduce data visualizations sooner in the presentation, focus on creating a "visual taxonomy"; very text heavy | Visually connect initial redlining map with later data visualization regarding surface temperature differences | Echoed sentiment about the amount of text; suggested practicing flow of presentation in order to decide key points to visually highlight through visualizations |
